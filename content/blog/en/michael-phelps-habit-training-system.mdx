# Michael Phelps' Habit System: What He Built Before He Ever Chased Gold

Set goals again this year and still haven't hit them?

Looking for some motivation?

Here's the thing: maybe you're not failing because you lack motivation.

Maybe you have goals, but no system.

So let me tell you about my favorite American swimmer, the greatest Olympic swimmer of all time, Michael Phelps, and why systems matter more than goals.

## 1. Goals vs. systems: what's the difference?

Since we were kids in school, we've heard the same advice:

*"Have a goal. Make it specific and measurable. Achieve it, no matter what."*

But behavioral science looks at it differently:

|                      | Goal                     | System                            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| What it is           | The result you want      | The process you repeat every day  |
| Example              | Lose 20 pounds this year | Walk for 30 minutes every morning |
| Where you're looking | Someday in the future    | Today                             |
| When it ends         | Once you reach it        | It keeps going                    |

Staring at the goal you want doesn't change the outcome.

Results only start to follow once small daily actions pile up and repeat until they become a system.

It sounds simple, but it's really hard. Honestly, I struggle with it too.

When I set a goal and start working toward it, if things don't go well and I don't see results right away, I lose steam. I quit halfway, or it just fizzles out, and eventually I can't even remember what the goal was.

That's why I believe that taking small actions every day and building them into a system is about the only way an ordinary person like me can keep moving toward a goal.

James Clear, author of *Atomic Habits*, puts it this way:\[1]

**"You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."**

No matter how high you set your goals, in the end, your results only reflect what you actually do every day.

## 2. Phelps' secret: it wasn't the goal, it was the routine

Michael Phelps. You know him, right?

28 Olympic medals, 23 of them gold. The most decorated Olympian in history.

I once watched a short interview clip where he said something like this:\[3]

> I went to the pool and trained every single day, without missing one. Sundays, my birthday, Christmas, New Year's. In 20 years of swimming, there were at least 100 days when I just wanted to stay in bed, sleep, and not do anything. But I never missed a single day at the pool. I believed that was the difference between a good athlete and a great one. Great athletes have to do the things they don't want to do, every time.

Isn't that incredible? How could he train every single day without fail, as if it were just part of his daily system? There must have been so many days he didn't want to, days he wanted to fall apart. I really believe it was that day-after-day accumulation that led to his greatness.

But even Phelps had a weakness.

From a young age, he struggled with his emotions and had a hard time calming himself down. In a sport where races are decided by hundredths of a second, that's a serious weakness.

So his coach, Bob Bowman, told him:

**"Play the videotape."**

He didn't mean sitting in front of a TV and watching a tape. What he meant was to picture a perfect race in his mind, from start to finish.\[2]

* The moment he dives off the starting block
* The feeling of hitting the water
* Pushing off the wall at the turn
* The rhythm of his breathing
* All the way to his fingertips touching the final wall

Phelps ran this "mental videotape" every day, before falling asleep and as soon as he woke up.

There were no days off from training, either. He was in the water on Sundays and on Christmas, when everyone else was resting. Watching interviews about Phelps, I honestly thought, "Yeah... I'm just a regular guy." Training through the weekends too? That's really hard.

## 3. When his goggles filled with water, his system proved itself

2008 Beijing Olympics. The 200-meter butterfly final.

Right off the start, Phelps' goggles began filling with water. By the end of the race, he could barely see.

An Olympic final, and you can't see where you're going. Most people would panic and fall apart.

But Phelps didn't panic. He had played that mental videotape so many times that his body already knew how many strokes it would take to reach the wall. He counted his strokes, kept going all the way to the finish, and won gold, setting a world record along the way.\[2]

What Coach Bowman instilled in Phelps wasn't some grand goal.

It was a small routine repeated every day, and the "small wins" that piled up one by one.

## 4. Real motivation comes from building a system

To anyone out there searching for motivation, here's what I want to say.

Honestly, I'm saying it to myself too.

**"Goals are temporary. Systems change your life."**

* **Goal**: what you need to win the game
* **System**: what keeps you playing the game

Phelps isn't great because of the goal of winning gold medals.

He's great because of the routine he repeated every single day, until he was dead tired. Because of his system.

If Phelps had his mental videotape, we have **our own records**, the notes we write down every day about our lives and habits.

When you log a small habit every day, this is what happens:

**You log it → You realize, "I'm actually doing this" → You start to believe in yourself → Your mood can go up and down, but you don't get shaken → "It finally becomes your system"**

I log my morning workout in MOAVO Habits every day, too.

Even on days I have zero motivation, when I see the dots stacked up on my calendar, I think, "I'm still someone who keeps showing up," and I head out again the next morning. It's not my mood that moves me. It's my record.

## 5. Today's takeaway: lower the goal, start with the system

If you're looking for motivation, don't raise your goals.

Lower them instead, and start building a system with one small habit.

And remember: today's small win builds tomorrow's big change.

| Instead of this goal               | Start with this system                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Read 50 books this year            | Read 2 pages before bed                    |
| Lose 10 pounds in 3 months         | No eating after 8 p.m.                     |
| Become fluent in English           | Study English for 20 minutes on my commute |
| Finally start a business this year | Spend 1 hour on my business after work     |

Then write down just one line.

Like Phelps' videotape, that record will hold you steady on the days you start to waver.
