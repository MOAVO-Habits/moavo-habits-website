# Non-Technical Founder Story: How I Built a Habit App When I Felt Stuck in Life

## Why I built MOAVO Habits

There was a time when my days were packed, but I had no idea where my life was actually going.

I worked at my company, ran a bakery with my wife for years, and raised our two kids. Just making a living and getting through the day took everything I had. Somewhere in there, I wanted to build something of my own: a business, a system, a brand.&#x20;

**But every time, I'd start looking into it, and then stop.**

I felt unprepared. I didn't have enough money. Every time I looked into a new idea and then shelved it because it wouldn't work, I lost a little more confidence. I didn't want to keep living the same way, but I didn't even know what to change first.

What helped me through that time was simple: **writing down what I actually did today, and finding one thing I could do next from those notes.**

Of course, writing things down didn't solve my problems.

But instead of being filled with a vague sense of disappointment and self-blame that nothing was moving, I could see, in data and graphs, the small things I was actually getting done.

**That experience is where MOAVO Habits began.**

## 1. For the first time, I could see my days

It started with advice from a close friend: "Why not look for new possibilities in the U.S.?" That got me interested in health and everyday behavior. I wanted to know how people actually keep doing the things they know are good for them.

So I started studying behavioral science and habits, and I began trying out habit tracking apps already on the Appstore. I didn't expect much at first. But as I logged small actions every day, the days that used to slip by started to become visible. **What I did, what I missed, whether I was actually being consistent.** I could see how those records were adding up, right there in the data.

When I showed the app I was using to people around me, one of my friends asked for its name and tried it themselves. That didn't prove there was a business in it. But it was a clue that I wasn't the only one who found this interesting. So I started to wonder:

**What if I built my own habit app, one that really captured the value I'd found in keeping a record?**

## 2. Small notes came before big confidence

Of course, I had never built an app in my life. I didn't know how much App development would cost or what documents I'd need. I didn't have enough money for planning, design, development, marketing, or operations either.

So I hesitated many times, and sometimes I let it go completely. But I kept writing down everything I learned about app development in the habit app I was using, almost like a business notebook.

When I read it back later, something surprised me. Right next to the problems I hadn't solved were the things I'd already figured out. I understood terms that had meant nothing to me at first. Screens that once existed only in my head had slowly started to take shape.

Keeping a record wasn't a magic spell that created confidence out of nowhere. **It was more like a map showing how far I'd come and what was left.**

James Clear says that rereading old entries can reveal meaning you couldn't see at the time.\[1] Looking back, that's exactly what happened to me.

## 3. I broke "I'm going to build an app" into things I could actually do

To save money, and to explain my ideas clearly to developers, I started by learning what I could do myself.

I learned Figma through online courses and sketched out the first screens, then refined the UX design with help from a designer. I worked through the requirements and a detailed spec by talking it over with AI. Of course, I wasn't good at it at first. I just made something, fixed it, made it again, and fixed it again.

| The vague goal                      | What I actually did                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| I want to build a habit app         | Mapped out the features and user flow       |
| I want to design the app screens    | Drafted screens in Figma and revised the UX |
| I want to develop real app service  | Wrote a detailed spec and development brief |
| I want to explain my idea to others | Presented the project at an alumni meetup   |

When I presented my plan for MOAVO Habits at a graduate school alumni meetup, the response wasn't as warm as I'd hoped. One alum suggested I look at Duolingo. When I tried it, I felt small all over again. Something this well made already existed.

But over time, my question changed. **What could I learn from Duolingo?** Could I add something to the habit logging flow that makes people want to come back tomorrow?

**"Let's blend a familiar way of tracking habits with an experience that's actually fun!"**

That one idea got my stalled plan moving again.

## 4. In the hardest moment, my family was there

To keep development costs down, I put everything I had into a very detailed development plan and posted a project on a development platform. I met with several companies and chose a great development partner. I decided to use part of some money I'd just set aside for the initial development cost.

Then, right around that time, something unexpected happened, and the bank suddenly asked me to repay a large loan. The challenge I was just about to take my first step into looked like it would stop again. I searched everywhere for a way to raise the money, and I asked my family for help too.

That's when my wife told me:

**"Don't give up on what you've been preparing because of this."**

And she carried that weight with me.

That's why I can't tell this story as if it were only about my own persistence. My family, my development partner, the people who helped with design, and the people who gave me advice were all part of it.&#x20;

My records kept my thinking from breaking off, but I didn't do this alone.

## 5. We made it to launch. But I'm still looking for answers.

After plenty of ups and downs, we finished building the first MVP of MOAVO Habits and launched it on both iOS and Android on July 24, 2026.

| Back when I was preparing                     | Where it's led so far                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| I started by learning how apps get built      | Built the app with a development partner    |
| I put the screens and features into documents | It became a product people can actually use |
| I felt that keeping records helped me         | Now finding out whether it helps others     |
| I was the only user                           | Other people use it with me now             |

Launching is an important step, but it doesn't mean the business has succeeded. Not even close. There's still a long way to go.

Why people use it, where it frustrates them, what value they find in it: I still have a lot of things to learn and improve. But I have something the old me didn't. A service that once lived only in my head now actually exists. And every thought, worry, and realization from building it is still there, kept as a record.

Here's what I wrote in my own MOAVO Habits journal:

**"Even if other people laugh at it, I think I really did build something huge."**

And on the day we reached 100 users, I wrote:

**"Ah... still a long way to go. But let's keep going."**

## 6. Who I want MOAVO to be there for

Near the end of development, I was out on a morning run, watching a video about putting your whole heart into the rest of your life, and I started crying. I don't know why I cried. When I thought about why it hit me so hard, it became a little clearer who I wanted to build this for.

The people I pictured using MOAVO Habits weren't people who already have everything figured out.

**People who live busy lives but feel stuck in place.**

**People who tried something and stopped.**

**People who want to start again but don't know where to begin.**

**People like me. I hope it makes a real difference in their lives.**

I want MOAVO Habits to be a tool that stays by their side as they log today's small actions, look back, and choose their next step.  And looking back at yourself honestly through your records, then changing direction, can be a good choice too.

## 7. What's one line you could write down today?

**It's okay if your next plan isn't clear yet.**&#x20;

Just write down one thing from today.

Something like this:

* ***"Looked up one thing I was curious about. Next time, I want to dig deeper into this part."***
* ***"Tried it, and it doesn't fit my life right now. Decided to scale it down."***
* ***"I was worn out today, so I rested"***

Read them again a few days later, and you can see what you did, where you got stuck, and what help you needed. Your notes won't guarantee answers, but they can become material for your next choice.

## 8. Start keeping your own record with MOAVO Habits

You can start with a paper notebook or a notes app. **MOAVO Habits** is an app where you capture today's actions and thoughts as a short habit journal, look back on them as dots on a calendar, and get comments from AVO, your AI coach.

I built MOAVO Habits because I hope other people can get what I got from keeping a record.

**Today's small action, a hard moment, something you want to try again.**&#x20;

**Just write down one line.**
